60+ Names for Sugar on Ingredient Labels

H
Heather McKenzie · NASM Certified Nutrition Coach
July 31, 2026 8 min read

Sugar hides under 60+ different names on ingredient labels. Most people only recognize one.

Food manufacturers aren't lying when they say a product has "no sugar added." They're just using one of the other names for sugar on labels that most shoppers don't recognize. There's no legal requirement to use the word "sugar" itself — dextrose, maltose, and rice syrup all show up on the nutrition label as "added sugars," but on the ingredient list they can look completely unrelated.

That's the gap this list closes. Below is a categorized reference of the most common names sugar hides under — organized the way they actually show up on labels, not alphabetically. Bookmark this page, or scan any label directly with Sugar Scanner to get an instant breakdown instead of cross-referencing a list by hand.

Why Sugar Has So Many Names

Chemically, most of these are some combination of glucose, fructose, and sucrose extracted from different plant sources — cane, beets, corn, rice, coconut, dates, maple. The source changes the name. The effect on blood sugar mostly doesn't. A few, like brown rice syrup and dextrose, actually spike blood sugar faster than table sugar itself despite sounding gentler.

The Three That Sound Healthiest but Aren't

Not every name on this list behaves the same way in your body, and a few are worth knowing by name specifically because they're marketed as the "clean" alternative:

⚠️ Sounds Healthy, Isn't

Brown rice syrup — glycemic index of 98, nearly identical to pure glucose, common in "clean label" bars and organic granola.

Maltodextrin — glycemic index of 85–105, higher than table sugar, and it's not even always listed as a sweetener. It's used as a thickener or filler in protein powders and "healthy" snacks.

Organic invert cane syrup — still pre-digested sugar that absorbs directly into your bloodstream. "Organic" describes how it was grown, not how your body processes it.

How to Spot Hidden Sugar Fast

  • Check ingredient position, not just the name. Ingredients are listed in order of quantity — whatever's first is the most. If any sugar name shows up in the first three ingredients, it's a primary ingredient, not a minor one.
  • Watch for more than one sugar name at once. Manufacturers sometimes split sugar across two or three of these names so no single one has to appear near the top of the list, even though combined they outweigh everything else.
  • Don't trust "natural flavor" alone. It's a legal catch-all that can include sugar-derived compounds without naming them directly.
  • "No added sugar" isn't the same as low sugar. A product can be free of added sugar and still carry a high load of natural sugar from concentrated fruit juice or purée.
